Federated Cognitive Causal Graphs for Storm Overflow Accountability in England

England’s rivers and bathing waters have become a public test of whether digital monitoring can produce real accountability. This essay proposes a Federated Cognitive Causal Graph (FCCG): a platform in which each water company, the Environment Agency (EA) and the Met Office keep their data where it is, jointly learn a shared graph of what causes storm overflow spills, and expose that graph to regulators and the public through a constrained AI assistant that answers “what if” questions.
FCCG integrates three established ideas. The first is federated learning, in which computation travels to the data rather than data travelling to a central store. The second is causal discovery, which learns directed cause-and-effect structures rather than correlations; recent peer-reviewed work shows these approaches can be combined, allowing organisations to learn a shared causal graph by exchanging only parameters or summary statistics (Gao et al., 2023; Li et al., 2024). The third is a generative-AI “cognitive” layer that translates graph queries into plain English.
The solution sits within three Week 1 trends: AI/ML moving from prediction towards decision support, computation shifting towards the edge, and responsible innovation, where privacy and accountability are design inputs rather than afterthoughts. The problem also matches the author’s aspirational role in data platforms for regulated infrastructure.
